## When Endgame Mode appears

TileTactician treats the bag as effectively empty when the total number of
unseen tiles—everything not on the board and not in your rack—is **7 or
fewer**. At that point those remaining tiles fit on the opponent's rack, so the
app can infer that rack from the standard distribution.

This is a threshold heuristic, not a requirement that the counted total be
exactly zero. If more than seven unseen tiles remain, the app cannot infer the
opponent's rack with certainty and the Endgame panel stays hidden.

## What the panel shows

When the threshold is reached, the manual editor renders **Endgame Mode** with:

- **Your rack** — your current tiles, including blanks;
- **Opp. rack** — the inferred opponent tiles, with blanks displayed as `?`;
- **Endgame Analysis** — a button that runs the endgame solver; and
- a progress state while the solver is working, including an option to cancel.

The panel does not start a solve merely because the threshold is reached. Tap
**Endgame Analysis** to calculate the move list.

## Reading the results

Results are ordered by the solver's `netScore` (`yourScore −
opponentBestScore`). The panel lists up to 15 moves. Each row includes:

- the word, with blank assignments rendered in lowercase;
- its starting coordinate and direction;
- **net** — your score minus the opponent's best reply score; and
- the detail values `yourScore − opponentBestScore`.

The results header also reports the solver's elapsed time in milliseconds. A
positive net score is styled as an advantage; a negative net score is styled as
a disadvantage. If the solver finds no valid move for the current rack and
board, the panel says so instead of showing an empty ranking.

Tap a move row to apply that endgame placement to the manual board. You can tap
**Re-analyse** after changing the position.

## Why the rack can be inferred

The tile tracker starts with the standard 100-tile distribution and subtracts
tiles seen on the board and in your rack. Once seven or fewer tiles remain in
the unseen total, the implementation treats all of them as the opponent's rack.
If the total is zero, the inferred opponent rack is empty.

That inference is only available while the remaining count fits on a rack. It
is separate from a full-game score projection: the panel reports candidate
moves, their best replies, net scores, and elapsed solve time rather than a
win-probability forecast.

For the probability that individual letters remain available before this
threshold, see [Tile bag & tracking](/tiletactician/tile-bag).
